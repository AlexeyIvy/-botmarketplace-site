## 1. Research question

Does an unusually large one-hour BTCUSDT perpetual price move exhibit enough next-hour reversal to retain a material positive edge after conservative taker fees and execution-friction proxies across multiple market regimes and untouched recent history?

This is a **short-horizon intraday** experiment. It must not be called a true scalping backtest.

---

## 2. Economic hypothesis

Large short-horizon moves can contain a temporary component generated by:

- forced liquidations / stop cascades;
- temporary inventory imbalance;
- short-lived liquidity withdrawal;
- overshoot around abrupt information arrival.

If liquidity replenishes after the forced component, a fraction of the move may reverse over the next hour.

### Falsification case

The hypothesis should fail if:

- extreme hourly moves are predominantly informed / persistent;
- post-shock momentum exceeds reversal;
- the gross reversal is too small relative to realistic costs;
- the effect exists only in one historical era;
- modest latency or parameter movement destroys the effect.

No rescue filters are permitted after results are seen.

## 4. Frozen signal definition

For every eligible fully closed hourly perpetual bar `t`:

1. Define one-hour simple return:

   `r_t = close_t / close_(t-1) - 1`

2. Define trailing volatility using **only the 24 hourly returns strictly preceding `r_t`**:

   `sigma_t = sample_std(r_(t-24), ..., r_(t-1))`

3. Primary threshold:

   `K = 2.00`

4. Signal after bar `t` closes:

   - if `r_t <= -K * sigma_t`: **LONG reversal signal**;
   - if `r_t >= +K * sigma_t`: **SHORT reversal signal**;
   - otherwise: no trade.

If `sigma_t` is missing, non-finite, or zero, no signal is allowed.

The shock return `r_t` is deliberately excluded from the volatility estimator used to judge whether that same shock is extreme.

No volume, RSI, moving average, regime filter, funding filter, time-of-day filter, stop-loss, take-profit, or second indicator is allowed in the primary rule.

---

## 5. Frozen execution rule

### Primary execution

A signal becomes observable only after bar `t` is fully closed.

Therefore:

- enter at the **open of bar `t+1`**;
- hold exactly one hourly bar;
- exit at the **close of bar `t+1`**;
- long after a negative extreme;
- short after a positive extreme.

No same-bar entry is allowed.

No intrabar stop / target is allowed because hourly OHLC cannot reveal reliable event ordering inside the bar.

Back-to-back trades are allowed, but overlapping positions are not. Because the primary holding period is exactly one bar, a new trade can begin only when the prior trade has ended.

If the next required entry/exit bar is absent because of a data gap, the trade is skipped and logged rather than synthetically filled.

### Position size

Normalized research account:

- NAV starts at 1.0;
- when a signal is active, gross position notional = 100% of current NAV;
- no leverage above 1.0x;
- otherwise capital is cash with 0% research return.

This normalization is for edge testing, not a recommendation to trade the whole account.

### Liquidation / margin

The experiment uses no leverage above 1x and does not intentionally borrow. Any historical bar implying an adverse move so extreme that the simple normalized no-leverage model becomes economically invalid must be flagged and treated as an implementation failure rather than silently clipped.

Production liquidation thresholds / maintenance-margin tiers are outside E001 and would be required before any real deployment.

## 6. Funding treatment

Use actual available Binance BTCUSDT funding timestamps/rates when a perpetual position genuinely spans a funding timestamp.

Causality convention:

- include a funding event only when `entry_time < funding_time <= exit_time`;
- if funding timestamp equals the entry open exactly, assume the position was established after that funding event and do not attribute it to the new trade.

Funding sign:

- positive funding: long pays, short receives;
- negative funding: long receives, short pays.

Because the primary trade runs from one hourly open to that hour's close, funding contribution is expected to be small / often zero, but it must still be calculated rather than assumed away.

## 7. Frozen cost model

Historical bid/ask is unavailable, so execution friction is an explicit proxy. No maker rebate or queue advantage is assumed. All fills are treated as taker-style research fills.

### Fee component

Baseline research taker-fee proxy:

- **5 bps per side**.

This is a frozen research assumption, not a claim that every historical Binance account tier charged exactly this fee.

### Spread/slippage proxy

Applied adversely on both entry and exit:

| Track | Fee / side | Spread+slippage proxy / side | Total / side | Round trip |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| LOW | 5 bps | 1 bp | 6 bps | 12 bps |
| BASE | 5 bps | 3 bps | 8 bps | 16 bps |
| STRESS | 10 bps | 5 bps | 15 bps | 30 bps |

The STRESS track deliberately doubles the fee component and worsens execution friction.

Gross edge and each cost component must be reported separately.

A candidate whose average BASE net edge is of the same order as plausible cost-model error is not considered robust even if total P&L is positive.

## 14. Statistical uncertainty

For the primary `K=2.00`, BASE-cost path, estimate uncertainty on OOS net edge using a **calendar-week block bootstrap** so that nearby trades are not treated as fully independent.

Frozen bootstrap specification:

- unit: UTC calendar week;
- sample weeks with replacement;
- 10,000 bootstrap replications;
- RNG seed `1003`;
- statistic: average net P&L per executed trade in sampled weeks;
- report two-sided 95% percentile interval.

Weeks with zero trades remain part of the time structure but contribute zero trade count; bootstrap draws with zero total trades are discarded and counted.

No alternative bootstrap method may replace this one because its confidence interval looks better.

## 15. PASS / FAIL gates

The experiment can receive `PROMISING_SCREEN` only if **all mandatory gates** below pass for the frozen primary rule `K=2.00`.

### Gate D — Data integrity

PASS only if:

- source snapshot identity is recorded;
- UTC ordering is monotonic after deduplication;
- only fully closed bars are used;
- required entry/exit gaps are skipped, not interpolated;
- no look-ahead / same-bar entry is found.

Any causality defect => `FAIL` until implementation is corrected **without changing economic rules**.

### Gate N — Net edge magnitude

At BASE costs:

- VALIDATION average net edge/trade >= **+5 bps**;
- FINAL average net edge/trade >= **+5 bps**.

This margin is deliberately above zero because a tiny edge is not sufficient under coarse execution data.

### Gate C — Cost robustness

At STRESS costs:

- combined OOS period (2023-01-01 onward) average net edge/trade > 0;
- FINAL average net edge/trade > 0.

If modestly worse costs erase the edge, E001 cannot be promoted.

### Gate S — Statistical uncertainty

For combined OOS (2023-01-01 onward), BASE costs:

- the 95% week-block-bootstrap lower bound for average net edge/trade must be > 0.

### Gate PF — Trade quality

For combined OOS, BASE costs:

- profit factor >= **1.10**;
- at least **100 executed trades** total in combined OOS;
- at least **30 trades** in the FINAL slice.

If the event is too rare to reach these counts, classify the evidence as insufficient / weak rather than extrapolating.

### Gate L — Latency robustness

With the frozen one-hour delayed-entry stress and BASE costs:

- combined OOS average net edge/trade > 0.

### Gate P — Parameter neighborhood

At BASE costs on combined OOS:

- `K=1.75` net edge/trade > 0;
- `K=2.25` net edge/trade > 0.

This is a stability check, not a parameter-selection contest.

### Gate M — Missed-trade robustness

At BASE costs on combined OOS:

- 10% omitted-trade variant net edge/trade > 0;
- 25% omitted-trade variant net edge/trade > 0.

### Gate Y — Temporal breadth

Among completed OOS calendar years 2023, 2024, and 2025 at BASE costs:

- at least **2 of 3** must have positive net P&L;
- no single completed year may contribute more than **60%** of the sum of positive completed-year OOS P&L.

Partial 2026 is reported but is not used in this completed-year concentration gate.

## 16. Verdict mapping

### `FAIL`

Use when any of the following occurs:

- causality / data gate fails;
- combined OOS BASE net edge <= 0;
- FINAL BASE net edge <= 0;
- combined OOS STRESS net edge <= 0;
- bootstrap lower bound <= 0 **and** the point estimate is not economically material.

Do not rescue with new filters.

### `WEAK`

Use when gross/base economics are positive but one or more mandatory robustness gates fail, including insufficient trade count, weak cost margin, unstable neighbors, latency failure, or excessive year concentration.

Do not promote to forward.

### `PROMISING_SCREEN`

Use only if every mandatory gate D/N/C/S/PF/L/P/M/Y passes.

This is still historical evidence, not proof.

### `ROBUST_HISTORICAL_CANDIDATE`

E001 alone cannot award this status. It additionally requires a separate implementation/granularity audit and independent review of the backtest engine/results.

### `ADVANCE_TO_FORWARD`

Not available directly from the initial E001 run. It requires a subsequent explicit decision document after all historical and implementation gates pass.
